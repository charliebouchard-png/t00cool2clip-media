# Views playbook (research 12 Nov 2026) — the rules every video follows

Sources studied: YouTube Shorts product lead Todd Sherman (Creator Insider), Jenny Hoyos (600M Shorts views/yr; Creator Science interview), Paddy Galloway's 3.3B-view / 5,400-Shorts study, MrBeast's production memo, Kallaway (hooks, 1M followers), Logan Smith (retention), the Claude-built 3D shorts channel breakdown (200M views/30 days), TikTok Creator Rewards rules, TikTok + YouTube AI/originality policies, short-form sound-design guides. Links at the bottom.

## 1. How the feeds decide
- Both apps test a new video on a small seed audience, then widen or kill it based on how that audience reacts. "The audience is the algorithm."
- YouTube: "viewed vs swiped away" is the gate. Under ~40% almost never passes 10k views; 70–90% opens the door to 100k+. Then average view duration: Shorts averaging 50–60 s watched averaged ~4M views in Galloway's study.
- TikTok: watch time + finish rate + rewatches first; shares/saves/comments count most after a full watch. Search value now matters (keywords spoken, on screen and in the caption).
- No time-of-day or "post X a day" bonus (YouTube, on record). TikTok itself recommends 1–4 posts/day; quality beats volume.
- Never delete-and-repost a flop (flagged as spam). Learn, make a better version.

## 2. Length
- TikTok rewards dense 10–40 s videos for reach; YouTube rewards 50–60 s stories for watch time. Creator Rewards pays only for 60 s+ originals, and needs 10k followers + 100k views/30 days — we're not there yet, so reach comes first.
- Our mix: 3 videos/day at 35–45 s (target "tiktok"), 2 at 55–65 s (target "youtube"). Re-check weekly against our own stats.
- Every second counts: trimming 1 dead second at the end took one of Jenny's Shorts from 83% to 88% retention and it took off. End the instant the payoff lands.

## 3. The script formula (every video)
1. **Hook (0–1.5 s), 1 line ≤12 words:** a paradox or shocking specific claim with topic clarity + on-target curiosity. Say "you/your". Contrast A vs B ("Ryanair sells £15 flights. It still made €2 billion."). No "hey guys", no "let's get started", no vague "you won't believe this".
2. **Foreshadow (≤3 s):** promise the payoff and the mechanism ("…three tricks, and the last one is sneaky"). The viewer must know what they're waiting for.
3. **Tension stack:** 2–3 escalating details. Use but/so/therefore, never "and then". Stair-step: each beat bigger than the last.
4. **Pivot:** "You see…" / "Here's the trick…" then the explanation, told as a story with a person in it.
5. **Twist in the last second**, then stop dead. The last line runs straight back into the first line, so the replay feels seamless.
6. Reading level ≤ grade 5–6: short words, active voice, explain jargon instead of using it (say "money left over" not "profit margin").
7. One comment trigger per video: a poll ("Would YOU pay that?") or a deliberately debatable claim. Never fake comments or fake accounts.

## 4. Visual rules
- First frame must explain the video **with the sound off**: hook card + the place + the presenter pointing at it.
- Something changes every 2–3 s: camera glide to a new angle, a number card, a badge, an emoji burst. The camera glides (ease in/out ~0.8 s) between angles inside a scene and hard-cuts with a whoosh between places.
- Cartoon finish: bold outlines, contact shadow under the feet, blurred background for depth, warm light, no pure black.
- Captions every word, key numbers highlighted; keywords on screen for search.
- Rotate presenter, format, caption style, colour grade and music seed: never the same template look twice in a day (YouTube demonetises "templated/repetitive"; TikTok suppresses unoriginal content).

## 5. Sound
- Tick on the hook card, whoosh on every place change, soft swish on camera moves, pop on number cards, thunk on badges, boing on punchlines, ding on polls, a riser into the twist, and the music drops out for half a second right before the twist. Effects never louder than the voice.
- Voice: energetic, ~3.3–4 words/second, no pauses longer than 0.15 s inside a scene.
- Original music only (no trending copyrighted tracks on auto-posts).

## 6. Safety / originality
- Every video brand new: new script, places, presenter rotation. No reuploads, no copies across accounts, no watermarks.
- Our presenters are clearly cartoon, so TikTok's AI label isn't strictly required, but Charlie can turn it on (it's honest and protects the account). Never make a presenter look like a real person.
- YouTube won't monetise "AI personas giving financial advice": tell money **stories and explanations**, never "you should buy/invest". Facts verified with a source the same day.
- 3–5 specific hashtags; caption = one keyword-rich sentence; no #fyp spam.
